India mounts costly defence of rupee
FT Markets · 2026-10-22 12:00
查看英文摘要Show English excerpt
Central bank has spent about $90bn of money raised from diaspora to shore up currency
发生了什么What happened
India's central bank is mounting a costly defence of the rupee, having spent about US$90 billion — much of it raised from diaspora deposits — to shore up the currency.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
This exposes the composition of reserves: intervening with liability-type funding means borrowing dollars to defend the currency, and if that money leaves, the defence collapses from both sides.
金融传导Transmission
Downward pressure on the rupee builds→central bank sells dollars to buy rupees→FX reserves fall→it replenishes with NRI deposits and other liability-type funds→the market sees through the intervention capacity→depreciation expectations feed on themselves→import prices rise and imported inflation picks up→the central bank is forced to hike, hurting growth
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • Why are non-resident Indian (NRI) deposits a problem? They are liabilities — repayable at maturity and interest-bearing — not genuine owned reserves. Defending a currency with short-dated liabilities is a maturity mismatch.
  • How much reserve is enough? Two conventional gauges: import cover in months (typically three to six) and the Guidotti-Greenspan rule, where reserves should cover short-term external debt due within a year. Looking only at the headline total misleads.
  • Why does intervention get "seen through"? The market can calculate how many dollars you burn each month. Once the burn rate is predictable, speculators know where the line is and attacks concentrate.
  • Imported inflation transmission: currency depreciates → imported energy and intermediates get dearer → PPI rises → CPI rises → the central bank hikes → funding costs rise and investment slows. That is a complete depreciation tax.
  • A point for interviews: in a currency defence, the quality of reserves matters more than the quantity — borrowed dollars cannot defend a domestic currency.

Global stocks tumble as oil prices rise
FT Markets · 2026-10-22 20:12
查看英文摘要Show English excerpt
Crude prices jump 3.5% while Eurozone bond sell-off deepens
发生了什么What happened
Global equities tumbled as crude jumped 3.5% and the euro-area bond sell-off deepened, with risk assets and government bonds under pressure at the same time.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
Higher oil pushes inflation expectations up and growth expectations down at once — a stagflationary shock where stocks and bonds stop hedging each other and the classic 60/40 portfolio fails.
金融传导Transmission
Middle East tensions escalate→supply risk premium on crude rises→oil jumps 3.5% in a session→inflation expectations rise→expected rate cuts get pushed back→long-end yields rise→bond prices fall→a higher discount rate compresses equity valuations→stocks and bonds fall together, diversification fails
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • Why do stocks and bonds fall together? Normally higher oil helps energy shares and hurts consumers, leaving the index roughly flat. But when oil is read as "inflation → hikes", bonds sell off first and the higher discount rate then presses on equities, aligning both.
  • The 60/40 portfolio assumes negative stock-bond correlation. That correlation turns positive when inflation is volatile — as in the 1970s and in 2022 — which is the most painful regime for asset allocation.
  • A 3.5% one-day move is large for crude: daily volatility runs about 2% to 3%, so 3.5% is roughly one and a half standard deviations and usually needs an event, not a gradual shift in supply and demand.
  • Why is the euro-area bond market more fragile? Member states have differing fiscal capacity, so spreads widen in risk-off episodes and peripherals (Italy, Greece) pay more. Monetary union without fiscal union is the structural flaw.
  • A closing point: whether a shock is supply-side or demand-side determines the policy response — under a supply shock the central bank is trapped, since hiking fights inflation but kills growth, while not hiking lets expectations drift.

Ship reported on fire off Oman as Iran escalation fears mount
DW · 2026-10-29 18:04
查看英文摘要Show English excerpt

发生了什么What happened
A ship was reported on fire in the Strait of Hormuz, a British maritime agency said, while Iran claimed to have downed another US drone — deepening fears of escalation.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
Hormuz carries about a fifth of seaborne crude. Any evidence that transit is impaired immediately lifts freight and insurance, well before actual supply is lost.
金融传导Transmission
A vessel is attacked and catches fire in the strait→war-risk premiums jump→shipowners reroute or suspend voyages→effective capacity falls→crude and LNG freight rates rise→landed costs climb→Asian refinery margins are squeezed→refined product prices pass through to end users
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • The war-risk premium is an immediate variable: cover written on top of hull insurance, quoted per voyage, and it can multiply within days when conflict escalates. It reacts earlier than the oil price itself.
  • Why does rerouting not solve it? Hormuz has no alternative channel. Routing around the Cape of Good Hope works only for some voyages and adds 10 to 14 days, which consumes capacity.
  • LNG is more fragile than crude: almost all Qatari LNG exits through Hormuz, LNG carriers are expensive to divert, and Asia is the main buyer — which is why Asian and European gas prices are most sensitive to incidents there.
  • "Effective capacity" is the concept: longer voyages mean fewer completed trips from the same fleet, so freight rates rise. It is the same logic as ton-mile demand in tanker markets.
  • A point for interviews: the geopolitical route into inflation runs freight → landed cost → shelf price, which is slower than the oil price but more persistent.

金融传导Transmission
The EU and China reach an interim trade deal→disputes emerge over implementation and interpretation→both sides trade accusations and rhetoric escalates→companies cannot judge the final rules→investment and procurement decisions are postponed→supply chains keep building dual tracks→long-run costs rise and efficiency falls
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • Why is an interim deal unstable? It typically sets a tariff ceiling and a negotiating deadline without a dispute-settlement mechanism. The fight over interpretive authority at implementation is where the next round begins.
  • The damage to firms is not the cost but the uncertainty: capex needs three to five years to pay back, and if the rules change annually the NPV simply cannot be computed.
  • The cost of dual sourcing: two sets of capacity for the same output, weaker scale economies and higher unit costs — ultimately paid by consumers.
  • The structure of a "who blinks first" game: each side waits for the other to concede, but when domestic political pressure is high, conceding costs more politically than the economic gain, so the standoff persists.
  • A closing thought: the lasting effect of trade friction is not the tariff rate but the redrawing of global production networks, and that efficiency loss is permanent.

金融传导Transmission
AI demand drives repeated earnings beats for chipmakers→share prices price in 2027 peak earnings ahead of time→the valuation absorbs everything and the margin for error disappears→any delivery delay or order cut→earnings estimates are cut→both earnings and multiples compress→sector volatility rises
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • What does "peak earnings" mean? The market assumes a given year is the cyclical top and earnings fall afterwards. Applying a multiple to peak earnings treats the cycle high as perpetual.
  • The right way to value cyclicals: use mid-cycle earnings, or price-to-book rather than P/E. A low P/E on peak earnings looks cheap precisely when it is most dangerous.
  • Why does the surprise matter more than the level? Price already contains expectations, so only the deviation from expectations generates return. That is the pricing logic behind "buy the rumour, sell the news".
  • Three drivers of the semiconductor cycle: inventory (three to four years), capex (equipment), and technology generations (nodes and AI architectures). Volatility peaks when all three align.
  • A closing line: the biggest risk for chip stocks is not demand disappearing but demand being pulled forward — customers double-order to secure capacity, which overstates true demand.
