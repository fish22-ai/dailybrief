A global stock selloff triggered safe-haven buying, with the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield falling as much as 10 basis points to 5.09%, Treasuries leading gains and the dollar index moving lower.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
The market had been pricing in yields staying elevated amid resilient growth and sticky inflation; safe-haven flows into government bonds pushed yields lower, and global bonds, the dollar and equity risk premiums need to be repriced.

①Stock selloff→safe-haven demand rises→funds buy U.S. Treasuries→Treasury prices rise→10-year U.S. Treasury yield↓→the yield advantage of dollar assets declines→dollar index↓

  • Why does the 10-year U.S. Treasury matter? It represents the long-term risk-free rate Rf, affecting equity valuations, corporate bonds and mortgage rates.
  • Falling yields mean rising bond prices: bond prices and yield to maturity YTM move inversely; safe-haven buying pushes prices up and yields down.
  • Basis point: 1 basis point equals 0.01 percentage point, so 10 basis points is 0.10 percentage point.
  • Why did the dollar move lower? Falling Treasury yields weaken the relative appeal of dollar assets, slowing capital inflows and reducing demand for the dollar.
  • Safe-haven assets: when markets panic, funds flow to assets with better liquidity and credit quality; U.S. Treasuries, the yen and gold are often seen as havens.
  • Why did global bonds rise in tandem? U.S. Treasury yields are the global benchmark for the risk-free rate, and their decline pushes down other developed-market bond yields.
  • Note that 5.09% is still high: it shows the market had previously priced in long-term high rates, and safe-haven flows only pushed yields down in the short term.

In charts: How one of the world's biggest economies got trapped by debt
CNBC Economy · 2026-10-13 23:52
查看英文摘要Show English excerpt
France opens debate on its 2027 budget as high deficits, rising debt and student protests intensify pressure on lawmakers.
发生了什么What happened
France opened its 2027 budget debate, with high deficits, rising debt and student protests putting pressure on lawmakers.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
The market had viewed French sovereign risk as controllable, but a budget deadlock and rising debt could widen French government bond spreads, forcing a repricing of the euro and European bank stocks.
金融传导Transmission
①France's high deficit→budget negotiation deadlock→rising government bond issuance pressure→increased supply of French government bonds→investors demand a higher term premium→French-German spread widens→euro under pressure
②European banks hold French bonds→concerns over valuation losses rise→bank stocks under pressure
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis8 条8 notes
  • Sovereign debt trap: when government debt is high, interest expenses crowd out fiscal space, the market demands higher rates, refinancing costs rise, creating negative feedback.
  • French government bond spread: the difference between French government bond yields and German government bond yields, measuring how the market prices French credit and fiscal risk.
  • Why use German government bonds as the benchmark? German government bonds are seen as the lowest-credit-risk risk-free asset in the euro area and serve as the pricing anchor for other euro-area government bonds.
  • Term premium: the extra compensation investors require for holding long-term bonds rather than rolling short-term bonds; rising fiscal uncertainty pushes it higher.
  • Why is the euro under pressure? Rising fiscal risk weakens the appeal of euro assets, and capital may shift to the dollar or German government bonds.
  • Why are banks affected? European banks hold large amounts of their own government's bonds; falling bond prices lead to book losses and may tighten credit.
  • Why does the budget debate matter? If the deficit cannot be cut, issuance will be larger, and the supply-demand imbalance will push up financing costs.
  • Student protests: political pressure may make fiscal consolidation harder, and the market worries about stalled reforms.
