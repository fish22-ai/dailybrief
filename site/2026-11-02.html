查看英文摘要Show English excerpt
US tech stocks set to start week higher following bruising rout
发生了什么What happened
Nasdaq has just posted its worst month in over a year; US tech stocks opened higher on 2 November, easing the global AI sell-off for now.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
The market had priced AI capex as an uninterrupted growth story. This drawdown is a repricing of return uncertainty — the valuation anchor shifting from narrative back to the rate constraint.
金融传导Transmission
AI capex keeps expanding→market starts questioning the payback period→tech earnings estimates cut→Nasdaq posts worst month in over a year→trend and leveraged funds forced to de-risk→selling feeds on itself, losses amplify→valuations fall back to what rates can support→selling exhausts, index bounces technically
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • Why does a sell-off feed on itself? Trend-following and risk-parity funds size positions by volatility: vol rises → models demand less leverage → forced selling → vol rises again. This positive feedback is the volatility feedback loop, and it is the mechanical reason drawdowns feel bottomless even when fundamentals barely move day to day.
  • The link between valuation and rates can be written as
    P/E = 1/(r − g)
    a rearrangement of the Gordon growth model. Here r is the risk-free rate plus a risk premium and g is perpetual growth. If r rises and g does not, the justified P/E falls — so "rates are gravity for valuations" is not a metaphor, it is the denominator.
  • "Worst month" should be read as the cumulative decline measured on month-end closes. It captures sustained distribution; it is not the same as a single-day crash, which is a V-shaped panic. Persistent selling means capital is leaving in tranches, and repair is slower.
  • If AI capex returns disappoint, the loss does not flow through cost of goods — it flows through impairment. Longer assumed useful lives flatter current profits; admitting the mistake dumps several years of accumulated earnings into one quarter.
  • A good interview close: this is not "AI is over". It is that once the discount rate r moves up, assets whose cash flows sit far in the future must fall first — the same disagreement hurts value stocks far less than growth stocks.

Junk debt rout sparks ‘bloodbath’ for credit investors
FT Markets · 2026-11-01 22:54
查看英文摘要Show English excerpt
Aggressive tactics to shift assets beyond reach of creditors have added fuel to sell-off
发生了什么What happened
US high-yield (junk) bonds are being sold heavily after some issuers moved assets beyond creditors' reach; investors are calling it a "bloodbath".
市场为什么在意Why it matters
Credit is priced off expected recovery. Assets moved out of reach mean a lower recovery rate, so the same default probability demands a wider spread — and refinancing costs rise from there.
金融传导Transmission
Issuers move assets out of the collateral pool→fewer assets creditors can claim→expected recovery rate falls→investors demand wider credit spreads→high-yield prices drop, YTM rises→issuers' refinancing costs climb→weaker cash flow at low-rated firms→default probability rises further
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • A working approximation for credit spreads:
    y_corporate = y_treasury + Spread
    where
    Spread ≈ PD × (1 − recovery rate)
    PD is the probability of default and recovery is what creditors actually get back. The formula says lower recovery and higher PD push the spread the same way.
  • Why is the recovery rate decisive? Because a bondholder's expected loss is not "the whole principal" but
    principal × (1 − recovery)
    Move the assets and the claim still exists on paper, but at enforcement the collateral pool turns out to be empty — the core of recent liability-management plays.
  • What is liability management? Before an actual default, an issuer exchanges old notes for new ones with longer maturities and weaker security via an exchange offer. It is not legally a default but economically an out-of-court restructuring, and ratings agencies often downgrade only afterwards.
  • High yield is doubly rate-sensitive: higher risk-free rates raise the denominator while a slowing economy raises PD. Both bite at once, so drawdowns are often sharper than equities — and liquidity is worse, so bids vanish exactly when you want to sell.
  • Likely follow-up: which reacts first, junk bonds or stocks? Usually credit. A bondholder's upside is capped at the coupon and the downside is principal, so once recovery looks impaired you must cut — there is no "wait for the bounce" option.

展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • 信用利差的定价近似式：
    y_corporate = y_treasury + Spread
    而
    Spread ≈ PD × (1 − 回收率)
    PD 是违约概率，回收率是违约后债权人实际拿回的比例。这条式子说明：回收率下降和违约概率上升，对利差是同一方向。
  • 回收率（recovery rate）为什么关键？因为债券投资者的期望损失不是「本金全没」，而是「本金 × (1 − 回收率)」。资产被移走，法律上债权还在，但变现时发现抵押池是空的 —— 这是近年 liability management 操作的核心。

发生了什么What happened
On the final weekend before the US midterms, Trump campaigned hard in traditionally Republican states; polls show Tuesday's fight for Congress is unusually tight.
市场为什么在意Why it matters
Who controls Congress sets the feasible boundary for spending, taxes and regulation. The market had priced "split government = gridlock"; a one-sided result forces repricing of deficit and tariff risk.
金融传导Transmission
Midterms approach→control of Congress uncertain→fiscal and tariff path unclear→term premium on long-dated Treasuries rises→yield curve steepens→dollar and real yields strengthen together→capital outflow pressure on emerging markets→currencies of high-external-debt economies come under strain
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • What are the midterms? The congressional elections held midway through a president's four-year term (all House seats plus roughly a third of the Senate). The incumbent party has historically almost always lost seats, so it works as a mid-term report card on the president.
  • Term premium: the extra compensation investors demand for lending to the government for a long horizon, covering inflation uncertainty and fiscal sustainability. A wider deficit → more Treasury supply → higher term premium → higher long-end yields. This is the mechanism behind "fiscal risk shows up at the long end".
  • Why is gridlock good for bonds? A stalemate means new spending and tax cuts cannot pass, so deficit expansion slows. The election's bond impact therefore depends not just on who wins but on the margin — a landslide is the bad outcome for duration.
  • Why are tariffs an immediate variable? Tariffs can be adjusted by the executive branch under existing authority without new legislation. So the election's effect on trade policy can reach import prices and inflation expectations within days.
  • A crisp line for interviews: what gets priced is not the party but the size of the policy change. Uncertainty itself carries a price, and it shows up in term premium and implied option volatility.

发生了什么What happened
Iran warned that a renewed war with the US could last longer than the previous round, after Trump said more targets still needed to be "taken care of".
市场为什么在意Why it matters
The market had priced a limited conflict that cools quickly. A prolonged conflict changes the transit risk premium through the Strait of Hormuz, hitting crude and shipping costs first.
金融传导Transmission
US and Iranian rhetoric escalate→probability of a prolonged conflict rises→transit risk premium through Hormuz rises→front-month crude trades at a premium to back months→shipping insurance and freight rates rise→transport costs push core inflation higher→central bank rate cuts get pushed back→long-end yields rise, growth stocks under pressure
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • Why is Hormuz the pricing switch? About a fifth of seaborne crude passes through it, and alternatives (pipelines, routing around the Cape of Good Hope) have limited capacity. Any threat of closure shows up immediately in the Brent front-to-back spread.
  • Backwardation: front-month prices above deferred months means physical crude is tight — buyers pay up to get barrels now. It is the most direct read on supply risk, earlier and more honest than headlines.
  • Why does oil reach core inflation? Energy sits directly in the CPI energy component and also seeps in indirectly through freight, fertiliser and plastics. Central banks usually "look through" a one-off oil shock but watch second-round effects closely — that is, whether wages and services prices follow.
  • A geopolitical risk premium is not a change in supply and demand; it is insurance paid for uncertainty. It arrives fast and leaves fast: once tensions cool, the premium unwinds within days, so it is a poor basis for long-term positioning.
  • Worth adding in an interview: what determines the inflation path is not the oil price level but how long it stays there. The longer it lingers, the more it leaks into expectations, and the harder it is for a central bank to ignore.

市场为什么在意Why it matters
Data centres have shifted from asset-light operations to heavy assets plus high leverage, with cash flow dependent on long leases. If AI demand growth slows, the whole repayment assumption has to be revalued.
金融传导Transmission
AI training and inference demand surges→data-centre capex jumps→operators turn to syndicated loans and bonds→data-centre debt supply rises→rating agencies scrutinise lease concentration→funding costs rise and tenors shorten→project IRR falls→new capacity expansion slows
展开解析Show analysis收起解析Hide analysis5 条5 notes
  • A syndicated loan is underwritten by several banks each taking a share: large ticket size, typically 3 to 7 years, priced at SOFR + spread. It is faster than a bond deal and larger than a bilateral loan, which is why it is the standard tool for heavy-asset expansion.
  • The key project-finance metric is DSCR (debt service coverage ratio):
    DSCR =cash flow available for debt servicedebt service due
    with banks typically requiring 1.2 to 1.3 times or more. It measures how much cash flow can fall before repayment breaks.

  • Cross-shareholding: A holds B while B holds A, or companies under the same ultimate owner subscribe to each other's IPO allocations. It inflates the headline subscription multiple and hides how strong real demand actually is.
  • Free float is the proportion of shares genuinely available to trade. Too low and the price is easily moved by small amounts of capital, the stock gets excluded from some indices, and institutions cannot build a position — hence a liquidity discount in valuation.
  • Why do regulators care? Hong Kong specifically scrutinises shell companies and highly concentrated shareholdings (known locally as "wai fei"), to stop post-listing manipulation where a thin float is marked up and then distributed to retail at the top.
  • The economics of an IPO discount: the greater the uncertainty, the more discount underwriters must leave on the table to attract cornerstone investors. The discount is not "cheapness" — it is the price of information asymmetry.
  • A way to tie it together in an interview: primary-market structural problems do not disappear, they migrate into the secondary market as a liquidity discount. However good the first-day pop, a tiny float eventually pays that bill.

  • 交叉持股（cross-shareholding）：A 持有 B、B 又持有 A，或同一实控人旗下公司互相认购 IPO 份额。它会让「认购倍数」虚高，掩盖真实需求强度。
  • 自由流通量 free float：真正可在市场上交易的股份比例。太低会导致股价容易被少量资金拉动、被排除在部分指数之外，机构也没法建仓 —— 所以估值要给流动性折价。
  • 监管为什么在意？港股对「壳股」和股份高度集中（俗称围飞）有专门审查，防止上市后用少量筹码操纵价格，再高位派货给散户。
  • IPO 折价的经济含义：不确定性越高，承销商必须留出越大的折价来吸引基石投资者。折价不是「便宜」，而是对信息不对称的定价。
  • 面试可以这样串：一级市场的结构问题不会消失，它会以「流动性折价」的形式转移到二级市场 —— 上市首日涨得再好，流通盘太小的股票长期都要还这笔账。
